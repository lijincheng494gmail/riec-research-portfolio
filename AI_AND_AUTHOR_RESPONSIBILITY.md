# AI and author responsibility

AI tools assisted implementation, code review, language editing, literature organization and the mechanical assembly of research artifacts. This assistance substantially increased execution bandwidth.

The author retained responsibility for:

- selecting the scientific question;
- deciding which protocol and thresholds could be frozen;
- defining units, deployment worlds, evidence identities and claim families;
- checking code, numerical outputs and alternative explanations;
- deciding when a result was a bug, an artifact, a negative result or evidence;
- preserving failures and limiting claims;
- deciding when to continue or stop a research branch;
- approving the public record and accepting final scientific accountability.

The working principle is **Direction — Verification — Responsibility**. AI can generate candidate actions; it cannot replace the person who decides what evidence is trustworthy, where a claim must stop or whose name is attached to the conclusion.
