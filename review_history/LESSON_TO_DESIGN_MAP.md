# From external challenge to later design

This map distinguishes three evidential levels:

- **documented external challenge**: an issue explicitly raised during
  editorial or peer review;
- **author retrospective**: a later interpretation grounded in fixed project
  artifacts but not attributed to a reviewer; and
- **subsequent design response**: a feature implemented in a later project.

Chronology supports a development claim. It does not prove that rejection
caused every later idea or that a reviewer endorsed a later method.

| Earlier event or limitation | Evidence level | What remained unresolved | Subsequent design response |
|---|---|---|---|
| September 2025 Systems submission combined many deterministic modules but lacked stable metric semantics and independent validation | Author retrospective | A complete reproducible pipeline was being treated as sufficient authority for a broad claim | RIEC-L1 reduced the object to a finite evidence ledger and a declared conflict-resolution operation |
| Initial RIEC-L1 was challenged as a weighted combination of familiar BIC and grouped-CV ideas | Documented external challenge | The manuscript had not identified the new capability beyond inspecting two criteria | Published RIEC-L1 separated descriptive evidence from a predeclared decision map and exposed switching behavior |
| The key RIEC-L1 disagreement case was statistically inconclusive | Documented external challenge | A visually interesting disagreement could not support a superiority claim | The negative result was retained and the final claim was narrowed to conditional, reproducible conflict resolution |
| Factory R1 remained dependent on synthetic and limited industrial evidence after a substantial auditable rebuild | Documented external challenge | Reproducibility and simulation could not establish external industrial benefit | RIEC-Core later distinguished development evidence from frozen lockbox evaluation and prohibited performance from compensating for qualification failure |
| Factory guard thresholds and components lacked strong necessity evidence | Documented external challenge | Framework completeness did not prove that each guard was necessary | Core preserved scope waivers and negative ablations; Agent used targeted risk-activation environments to test mechanisms under declared conditions |
| Factory rejection left unresolved how evidence roles, deployment targets and claim authority should be separated | Author retrospective | A locally correct workflow could still support the wrong scientific statement | RIEC-BCI made evaluation worlds explicit; Core formalized protocol, evidence, action and claim boundaries |
| Multiple individually compliant agents could repeatedly use shared evidence | New Agent research question | Local ledgers did not necessarily account for system-wide adaptive multiplicity | RIEC-Agent introduced persistent identity-aware accounting across agents, claims, aliases and rounds |

## Chronological boundary

The Factory decision was issued on 2 August 2026. RIEC-Core and RIEC-Agent
were subsequently made public as preprints, and the RIEC-BCI journal manuscript
was submitted after transfer on 24 August 2026. The author records BCI, Core and
Agent as post-Factory work. Their claims must nevertheless stand on their own
experiments and artifacts, not on the fact that an earlier manuscript was
rejected.
